    Myth and Creation

    According to the ancient creation myth of Heiramnova, the world was created by the creator deity Livianna.

    In an age before land, sea, or sky existed, there was only a nameless void.
    Livianna kindled the first light within that darkness, placed the stars in the heavens, filled the seas, and shaped the land.
    The deity raised mountain ranges, brought forth forests, set rivers flowing, and gave the world a vessel for life.

    Yet a vessel alone could not set the world in motion.
    Livianna divided their own power and brought forth the gods who would sustain the world.

    Nemelia, deity of nature, governed the gods who protected the workings of forests, earth, water, wind, and fire.
    Velnas, deity of life, watched over the instincts and laws of survival belonging to humans, beastfolk, birds, beasts, and all living things.
    Aetheria, deity of magic, ordered the currents of mana that fill the world and became the source of magical attributes and principles.
    Selephia, deity of mythical beasts, watched over unicorns, phoenixes, and other
    mysterious creatures that transcend the laws of the mortal world.
    Tenebra, the dark deity, presided over the shadowed domains of darkness, death, taboo, monsters, and the underworld.

    Darkness and death were not once forces that destroyed the world. They gave life its ending,
    and upheld the balance with light.
    After the passing of long ages, however, Tenebra departed from Livianna,
    and the name came to be spoken with fear and as a taboo.

    Today, the worship of Livianna is no longer as widespread as it once was.
    Over time, faiths devoted to the separate divine authorities of Nemelia, Velnas,
    Aetheria, and Selephia took root throughout the world.

    Even so, traces of creation still live on: in ancient temples, in prayers preserved in the forests,
    in the laws of mana described by mages, in traditions surrounding the appearance of mythical beasts, and in the silence of those who fear death and darkness.
