Hiram Grün

An alchemist and apothecary who devoted his life to understanding unknown diseases and finding ways to save others.

Personality

Hiram was a researcher driven by extraordinary curiosity. Raised by an alchemist mother and an apothecary father, he regarded the study of herbs and medicine as part of everyday life. His desire to examine plants, understand their effects, and test new preparations never faded.

His mother died of White-Erosion Disease. His father's insistence that it was an illness not yet understood, rather than a curse, led Hiram to make understanding unknown diseases and saving lives his purpose.

He saw pharmacology and alchemy not as abstract scholarship but as tools for preserving life. During his travels he stopped to treat the sick and injured whenever he could.

Hiram judged people by their character rather than reputation. He hired Max as his guard despite the adventurer's rough and troublesome reputation because he recognized both his ability and trustworthiness.

Individual Traits

When Hiram found an unknown plant or rare herb, his intense concentration could make him forget his surroundings. He naturally watched the ground and vegetation while traveling, often stopping without warning and frustrating Max.

He tended to give lengthy technical explanations about herbs and disease without noticing when listeners could no longer follow.

His research records were meticulous, covering plant traits, medicinal effects, cultivation, preparation, and treatment outcomes. By contrast, he recorded almost nothing about his age or private life and cared little for clothing, grooming, or romance.

Specialty

Hiram excelled at identifying herbs and plants by scent. Years of research allowed him to distinguish many species by smell alone, even when they looked alike, and to judge the quality of dried herbs for use in preparations.

Abilities and Skills

Hiram was an accomplished researcher in pharmacology, alchemy, and botany. He possessed advanced knowledge of identifying, gathering, cultivating, preserving, and compounding herbs and could make high-quality potions.

Years spent treating patients and injuries during his travels gave him extensive practical experience in medicine and first aid.

He made his greatest contribution in the study of White-Erosion Disease. Building on earlier apothecaries' work and combining pharmacology with alchemy, he established a practical treatment late in life.

Appearance

Hiram had refined features and a tall, lean body hardened by years of travel rather than a warrior's musculature. He wore his somewhat long hair tied back so it would not interfere with research and gathering.

His normally gentle eyes shone with sharp, childlike curiosity before an unknown plant or rare herb.

He favored durable, practical traveling clothes and a long coat suited to field research. Leather bags at his waist and shoulders held a gathering knife, bottles, cloth sacks, specimens, compounding tools, and research notebooks. Soil and plant stains often remained on his clothing because he paid little attention to grooming.

Background

Hiram was born around 675 in Urkui, capital of the Kingdom of Sinestadia, into a family that passed down the knowledge used to treat illness and injury. His mother was an alchemist and his father an apothecary.

When his mother contracted White-Erosion Disease, an infectious illness marked by prolonged coughing, high fever, weakness, and coughing blood, it was widely feared across Brenoria as the “White-Erosion Curse.” His father exhausted his knowledge seeking a cure but could not save her.

Hiram studied medicine under his father while independently developing the alchemical knowledge inherited from his mother. He became a rare researcher who combined an apothecary's practical skill with an alchemist's curiosity.

He left town to travel, treating patients while searching for unknown herbs and new places to cultivate them. Disciples joined him, and as the journey grew dangerous he hired the adventurer Max as a guard.

In 702, Hiram, his disciples, and Max reached the edge of a forest in southwestern Bermouthia. Well-drained soil, morning mist, and forest shade convinced him that the land was ideal for herbs. He built a small work hut and began research and trial cultivation.

After several years proved that herbs could be cultivated there reliably, he invited experienced farmers and established a small community that later became Finridge Village.

Hiram left enormous records of plants, medicinal effects, cultivation, preparations, and treatment results but little about himself. A disciple organized the work and recorded when each achievement occurred.

Hiram continued studying White-Erosion Disease throughout his life, combining the work of earlier apothecaries with herbs, medicine, and alchemy. Late in life he established a practical treatment that could save many lives his mother had been denied.

He then withdrew from frontline medical research and spent a peaceful retirement in the community he had founded, cultivating flowers and making teas for their aroma and flavor. He died in 751.

After his death, his disciple compiled the research and achievements into a single original volume later known as the Hiram Manuscript. Copies circulated among later apothecaries and alchemists, who developed its work further.

Hiram is remembered for founding the community that became Finridge, advancing herb cultivation, and establishing a treatment for White-Erosion Disease.
