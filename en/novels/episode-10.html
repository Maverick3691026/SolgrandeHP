Episode Ten: Inquiry

From demon swords to masterwork blades and magical aids, the children’s curiosity leads them toward ever more questions.

Episode Ten: Inquiry

Story

Back in his room, Arvel opened the long-awaited second volume of The Tale of Hero Klaus.

It told the story of Klaus joining the army of the Principality of Ernest, passing through countless battles and encounters, and rising from an ordinary soldier to a man hailed as a hero.

He wanted to know what happened next.

That alone kept his hand turning the pages. Even after sleepiness crept over him, he continued reading, promising himself just a little more.

He could not remember how far he read that night.

At some point, Arvel fell asleep with the book resting beside him.

◇

“Arvel! Hurry up!”

The next morning, Milica’s voice ringing out from the garden woke him.

“Tora’s already here!”

“...Already?”

Morning seemed to have begun without mercy from the very point where his memories of the previous night ended.

He hurriedly got ready, only for Milica to scold him the moment he stepped into the garden. “You’re late!”

“Reading again?”

Tora laughed.

“Yeah.”

Even Kieval gave a wry smile at the answer, but Tora had something else on his mind.

“Oh, right, Mr. Kieval. Do you know about demon swords?”

“You too?”

“I saw one in a bookshop yesterday. What kind of swords are they?”

“There are all kinds of demon swords. Some hold the power of fire or lightning, and others are said to house spirits. I’ve never seen a real one myself, though.”

Whenever the subject was swords, Tora became serious.

Before anyone noticed, even Milica had fallen quiet.

“But demon swords aren’t the only special swords.”

“There are others?”

Arvel asked.

“There are. The world also has swords known as masterwork blades.”

“Masterwork?”

Milica repeated the unfamiliar word exactly as she had heard it.

“They may not have any special power like magic, but they’re exceptionally well-made swords. They might keep their edge, resist breaking, or sit naturally in the hand and be easy to wield. Some swords crafted with great care by skilled blacksmiths are used for years because of qualities like those.”

Milica thought for a moment, then looked at the sword at her father’s waist.

“Then is your sword a masterwork blade, Father?”

“Mine?”

Kieval burst out laughing.

“Ha! Mine is an ordinary sword Ruben forged for me in his spare time.”

“It’s ordinary?”

“It is. But it cuts properly and it’s sturdy. It’s more than enough for everyday use.”

Kieval seemed perfectly satisfied with that.

Once that conversation ended, the three children began their usual morning training.

◇

After training, Arvel opened the second volume again at the garden table.

Across from him, Tora and Milica were still preoccupied with masterwork blades.

“Do you think you can tell one just by looking?”

“I wonder.”

As Arvel turned the pages while listening to them, Sallis leaned out of a window of the house.

“All three of you, it’s almost time to go to the church.”

Only then did Arvel finally look up from his book.

Tora and Milica also seemed to have lost track of time while talking about swords.

“You three.”

Kieval called to them just as they were about to leave.

“If you’re that curious about masterwork blades, stop by Ruben’s place on your way back from the church. He knows far more about smithing than I do.”

Tora’s and Milica’s faces brightened.

Arvel left the second volume at home and headed to the church with them.

◇

Three mornings a week, the church in Finridge became a place of learning for the village children.

Father Cassian taught them reading, writing, arithmetic, and the knowledge they needed in their daily lives.

That day’s lesson was no different from usual.

Arvel worked steadily through the assigned problems, but Tora, who struggled with lessons, stopped to think whenever they reached arithmetic, while Milica grew increasingly distracted by whatever lay beyond the window as time passed.

Even so, Cassian never rushed them. He carefully taught each child at the pace of their own understanding.

At last, the lesson ended and the children began to head home.

Arvel was about to leave the church when he suddenly decided he wanted to ask Cassian as well.

“Father Cassian, do you know about demon swords?”

“Demon swords?”

Cassian thought for a moment.

“I know the name, but I don’t know much about swords. For something like that, I think you’d be better off asking your father or Ruben.”

“So it is the village chief after all.”

“Swords are outside my expertise, but I do know something about tools that assist magic.”

Arvel’s interest immediately shifted in that direction.

“What kind of tools?”

“For example, there are staves that enhance the effect of healing magic. Even when you expend the same amount of mana, a tool can help increase the healing effect. There are also pieces of equipment that temporarily supplement the wielder’s mana.”

“Do you use them too, Father Cassian?”

“When necessary.”

Arvel and Tora both stared at Cassian.

“Wait, Father Cassian, you can use healing magic?”

“I can.”

Perhaps amused by their surprise, Cassian laughed.

“At the Temple of Karad Valdea, anyone studying to become a priest is expected to learn elementary healing magic.”

“Priests heal people too?”

“We don’t normally specialize in treatment. But when a major disaster or accident injures many people at once, there may not be enough healers. At times like that, priests who can use healing magic also take part in treating the injured.”

Arvel was almost satisfied with the answer when another question occurred to him.

“But if you kept using magic, wouldn’t you run out of mana?”

“Of course. That’s why you can’t overdo it.”

Cassian’s voice grew a little more serious.

“When your mana falls too low, you become terribly tired or dizzy. If you keep using it, your body can lose its strength, and you may even fall unconscious.”

“What happens if it’s all gone?”

“That is called mana depletion.”

It was a term Arvel had never heard before.

“Normally, it gradually recovers if you rest properly. But if complete depletion continues for too long, it places a great burden on the body. In severe cases, it can even endanger a person’s life.”

Even Milica, who had listened in silence until then, now wore a serious expression.

“That’s why the person providing treatment must be careful not to use up all of their own mana. The staves and equipment I mentioned also help reduce that burden.”

“Because if they make healing magic stronger, you can heal someone with less mana?”

“Exactly.”

Cassian nodded.

“If you can treat people while preserving your own mana, you can help that many more of them.”

Arvel had only asked about demon swords, yet somehow the conversation had expanded into uses of magic he had never known about.

Power dwelling within a sword.

Tools that assisted a caster’s magic.

Though both kinds of objects were connected to magic, their purposes were entirely different.

“...That’s fascinating.”

The words slipped out, and Cassian smiled.

“You truly do love things you don’t know, Arvel.”

“I do. Every time I learn something, I want to know even more.”

Arvel wanted to keep listening, but Tora spoke to him from beside him.

“Arvel, we should get going to the village chief’s place.”

“Oh, that’s right.”

What sort of stories would they hear from a blacksmith this time?

Arvel thanked Cassian and left the church with Tora and Milica.
