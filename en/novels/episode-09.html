Episode Nine: Interest

Arvel and his companions visit the Adventurers Guild in Dorgaf and deepen their interest in magical swords.

Story

“Demon Sword?”

Tora tilted his head.

“What’s a demon sword?”

“No idea...”

Arvel also stared at the two words written on the cover.

“But judging by the name, it doesn’t sound like an ordinary sword. Father or Darius might know.”

“Huh...”

Tora looked back at the book in his hands with interest.

Only then did Arvel notice the title written on its cover.

The Complete Guide to Weapons.

“Tora, you were looking at a book like that?”

“Hm? Yeah. Looks like it has loads about swords too.”

As he spoke, Tora started to open the book.

“A book about weapons this time?”

At the voice, the two boys turned around.

The old woman who ran the shop was watching them from behind the counter with an amused look.

“What is this demon sword thing?”

Tora asked as he showed her the book.

“You’ll find that in the book.”

Tora immediately began turning the pages.

But after he had flipped through several, the shop door opened.

“Hey, I’m finished.”

The two boys turned at the familiar voice.

Darius had entered the shop.

“I’m done buying supplies. It’s about time we headed home.”

“Already?”

Tora looked down at the book in disappointment.

“Yeah. If we leave much later, it’ll be dark by the time we reach the village.”

“...All right.”

Tora gave the cover of The Complete Guide to Weapons one last look, then returned it to its shelf.

Arvel carefully adjusted his hold on the newly purchased second volume of The Tale of Hero Klaus.

“Thank you very much!”

“You’re welcome. Come again.”

Seen off by the old woman, the two boys left the shop with Darius.

“Oh! That’s right, Darius! What about the Adventurers Guild?”

“The Adventurers Guild?”

“I said I wanted to see it!”

Arvel looked up as he remembered it too.

“I’d like to see it too...”

“All right. We still have a little time, so we can stop by for a bit.”

Seeing the expectation on both their faces, Darius smiled as though he had no choice.

“Yes!”

After they had walked through Dorgaf for a while, they came to a stone building facing a square, somewhat larger than those around it.

A sign for the Adventurers Guild hung over the entrance, and people carrying swords and bows passed in and out.

“Whoa...!”

Tora could no longer hide his excitement.

They followed Darius inside and found many adventurers in the spacious hall.

Notice boards covered with requests lined the walls, and several men and women stood before them discussing something.

At a table in the back, armored men chatted over cups of ale.

“Hey! If it isn’t Darius!”

A booming voice suddenly rang out.

A large man seated at a table was waving to them.

“Long time no see! What’s this, thinking of becoming an adventurer again? Come over and have a drink!”

“Ha ha. Another time!”

“That’s no fun! You ought to take a job with us once in a while!”

“Give me a break. Running a shop suits me better.”

The man laughed heartily, then looked at the two boys beside Darius.

“And who are those two? Don’t tell me you’ve taken on apprentices now.”

“No, nothing like that. They’re children from the village. I brought them to see the guild today.”

“I see! Take your time and look around!”

The man laughed cheerfully and returned to his conversation with his companions.

“Darius, do you know that man?”

Arvel asked.

“Yeah. I knew him back when I was an adventurer.”

“Wow...!”

Arvel looked back and forth between Darius, whom he usually saw minding the village general store, and the adventurers before him.

Beside him, Tora was already captivated by the sword hanging from another adventurer’s waist.

“Hello, Darius. Are you here to post a request today?”

A young woman called to him from the reception counter.

“No, not today. These two said they wanted to see the Adventurers Guild.”

“Oh, I see. Hello.”

“Hello!”

When the boys returned her greeting, the woman smiled warmly.

“Do you ask people here to do work for you too, Darius?”

Darius nodded at Arvel’s question.

“Yeah. Sometimes I have them gather materials for the shop. I can’t manage everything by myself.”

“Besides gathering herbs and hunting monsters, adventurers also accept requests from shopkeepers.”

The receptionist gestured toward the notice boards along the wall.

“Those are the requests currently available. Adventurers choose work suited to their rank.”

“Wow...!”

Tora studied the notice boards with keen interest, but Arvel had something else on his mind.

“Um, are there adventurers who use magic too?”

“Yes, of course. Many adventurers specialize in magic.”

“Really!?”

“Hehe. Some take requests alongside swordsmen and archers, while others work in groups made up entirely of mages.”

Arvel looked around the hall once more.

Some wore armor, while others carried staves and dressed lightly. He could not tell who used what kind of magic, but the adventurers he had known only from books were now standing before his eyes.

That delighted him more than anything.

Meanwhile, Tora studied the requests posted on the board and asked the receptionist a question.

“What’s that rank thing you mentioned?”

“It’s a grade representing an adventurer’s ability and achievements. Everyone starts at G-rank, then advances through F, E, D, C, B, and A, with S-rank at the top. There are eight ranks in all. Requests have ranks too, and more dangerous work requires a higher one.”

“Huh... So do monsters have ranks too?”

“Yes. For monsters, ranks serve as a general measure of how dangerous they are.”

“...Then what about an Owlbear?”

“An Owlbear? An ordinary adult is considered D-rank, while a superior individual that has lived a long time and grown stronger is considered C-rank.”

“...C-rank.”

Tora fell silent for a while, his eyes still fixed on the notice board.

“Hey, it’s about time we left.”

Darius called to them.

“What? Already?”

“We agreed it would only be for a little while. If we stay any longer, it really will be dark before we get back.”

“...All right.”

Darius urged along Tora, who still seemed reluctant to leave, and Arvel bowed to the receptionist.

“Thank you!”

“You’re welcome. Come visit us again.”

With the woman smiling after them, the three left the Adventurers Guild.

Even outside, Tora’s excitement showed no sign of fading.

“Hey, Arvel! That was amazing!”

“Yeah! They even have mages!”

“I wanted to look at more of the requests.”

“I wanted to talk to one of the mages.”

Darius listened to the two of them and laughed with delight.

“Ha ha! If you liked it that much, I’ll bring you again sometime.”

“Really!?”

“Yeah!”

The boys exchanged delighted looks.

They never ran out of things to say about the Adventurers Guild on the way back, and Darius found himself bombarded with questions again and again.

Unlike that morning, the bed of the wagon was now loaded with wooden crates and burlap sacks from the day’s buying.

There was not much empty space, so Arvel and Tora sat side by side at the edge of the wagon bed.

Darius handled the reins from the driver’s seat, and the wagon slowly made its way through the streets of Dorgaf.

People passing by.

Rows of shops.

Carts rolling over the cobblestones.

Arvel looked once more at everything he had seen since morning.

Before long, the wagon passed through the town gate and emerged onto the road leading to Finridge.

The streets of Dorgaf gradually receded into the distance.

After a while, Tora, sitting beside him, called toward the driver’s seat.

“Hey, Darius.”

“Hm?”

“Do you know about demon swords?”

“Demon swords?”

Darius glanced back briefly.

“Yeah. I saw it in the bookshop. The book said ‘demon sword.’”

“Let me think...”

Still facing forward, Darius considered the question for a moment.

“I don’t know much about them myself... but apparently they’re swords imbued with the power of spirits or magic.”

“Spirits!? Magic!?”

This time Arvel leaned forward eagerly.

“Darius, tell us more!”

“I just said I don’t know much.”

Darius gave a wry smile.

“But they have the power of spirits in them, right? What kind of spirits? Does magic come out of the sword?”

“I don’t know, I don’t know. Don’t ask me.”

“Aww...”

“If you want to know about demon swords, ask Kieval. He’ll know more than I do.”

“My father?”

“Yeah.”

Arvel looked down at the book cradled in his arms, then at Tora beside him.

“Shall we ask him when we get back?”

“Yeah!”

Tora nodded vigorously.

The wagon continued along the road.

That morning, every unfamiliar sight had held the boys spellbound. On the way home, however, they gradually grew quieter.

Before they knew it, the sky had begun to turn red.

The westering sun shone between the trees, dyeing the road orange.

At the edge of the wagon bed, Arvel rested the long-awaited second volume of The Tale of Hero Klaus on his lap.

Beside him, Tora gazed thoughtfully at the passing scenery.

The wagon continued down the road at dusk, bound for Finridge.

By the time they reached Finridge, the sky was completely colored by evening.

The setting sun still lingered in the western sky, while thin trails of smoke rose from the village homes as supper was prepared.

As the familiar scenery drew near, Arvel, who had been captivated by the world outside all day, finally felt that he had come home.

The wagon continued through the village and eventually stopped in front of Darius’s general store.

“All right, we’re here.”

Darius climbed down from the driver’s seat.

Arvel and Tora jumped down from the wagon bed and reached for the crates stacked there.

“So, should I carry this one?”

“I’ll take this one.”

“No, no. It’s late, so you two go home.”

“But we helped this morning, didn’t we?”

“That was this morning. The sun’s already going down. I can handle this myself.”

Tora still looked as though he wanted to help, but Arvel glanced at the book in his arms.

“...Then shall we go home?”

“You just want to hurry up and read that book.”

“Yeah.”

Darius laughed aloud.

“Darius, thank you for today.”

Arvel bowed his head.

“Me too! It was fun!”

“Sure. Let’s go again sometime!”

After thanking Darius once more, the boys left the general store.

Arvel headed home after parting with Tora along the way.

“See you, Arvel!”

“Yeah. See you tomorrow.”

Arvel watched Tora run off with a wave, then continued home himself.
