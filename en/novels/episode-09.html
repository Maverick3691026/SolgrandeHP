Episode Nine: Interest

Arvel and his family become fascinated by the mysterious powers said to dwell within demon swords.

Story Information

Old Unknown

Episode Nine: Interest

Story

“Demon Sword?”

Tora tilted his head.

“What’s a demon sword?”

“No idea...”

Arvel also stared at the two words written on the cover.

“But judging by the name, it doesn’t sound like an ordinary sword. Father or Darius might know.”

“Huh...”

Tora looked back at the book in his hands with interest.

Only then did Arvel notice the title written on its cover.

The Complete Guide to Weapons.

“Tora, you were looking at a book like that?”

“Hm? Yeah. Looks like it has loads about swords too.”

As he spoke, Tora started to open the book.

“A book about weapons this time?”

At the voice, the two boys turned around.

The old woman who ran the shop was watching them from behind the counter with an amused look.

“What is this demon sword thing?”

Tora asked as he showed her the book.

“You’ll find that in the book.”

“Really?”

Tora immediately began turning the pages.

But after he had flipped through several, the shop door opened.

“Hey, I’m finished.”

The two boys turned at the familiar voice.

Darius had entered the shop.

“I’m done buying supplies. It’s about time we headed home.”

“Already?”

Tora looked down at the book in disappointment.

“Yeah. If we leave much later, it’ll be dark by the time we reach the village.”

“...All right.”

Tora gave the cover of The Complete Guide to Weapons one last look, then returned it to its shelf.

Arvel carefully adjusted his hold on the newly purchased second volume of The Tale of Hero Klaus.

“Thank you very much!”

“You’re welcome. Come again.”

Seen off by the old woman, the two boys left the shop with Darius.

◇

Unlike that morning, the bed of the wagon was now loaded with wooden crates and burlap sacks from the day’s buying.

There was not much empty space, so Arvel and Tora sat side by side at the edge of the wagon bed.

Darius handled the reins from the driver’s seat, and the wagon slowly made its way through the streets of Dorgaf.

People passing by.

Rows of shops.

Carts rolling over the cobblestones.

Arvel looked once more at everything he had seen since morning.

Before long, the wagon passed through the town gate and emerged onto the road leading to Finridge.

The streets of Dorgaf gradually receded into the distance.

After a while, Tora, sitting beside him, called toward the driver’s seat.

“Hey, Darius.”

“What is it?”

“Do you know about demon swords?”

“Demon swords?”

Darius glanced back briefly.

“Yeah. I saw it in the bookshop. The book said ‘demon sword.’”

“Let me think...”

Still facing forward, Darius considered the question for a moment.

“I don’t know much about them myself... but apparently they’re swords imbued with the power of spirits or magic.”

“Spirits!? Magic!?”

This time Arvel leaned forward eagerly.

“Darius, tell us more!”

“I just said I don’t know much.”

Darius gave a wry smile.

“But they have the power of spirits in them, right? What kind of spirits? Does magic come out of the sword?”

“I don’t know, I don’t know. Don’t ask me.”

“Aww...”

“If you want to know about demon swords, ask Kieval. He’ll know more than I do.”

“My father?”

“Yeah.”

Arvel looked down at the book cradled in his arms, then at Tora beside him.

“Shall we ask him when we get back?”

“Yeah!”

Tora nodded vigorously.

The wagon continued along the road.

That morning, every unfamiliar sight had held the boys spellbound. On the way home, however, they gradually grew quieter.

Before they knew it, the sky had begun to turn red.

The westering sun shone between the trees, dyeing the road orange.

At the edge of the wagon bed, Arvel rested the long-awaited second volume of The Tale of Hero Klaus on his lap.

Beside him, Tora gazed thoughtfully at the passing scenery.

The wagon continued down the road at dusk, bound for Finridge.

By the time they reached Finridge, the sky was completely colored by evening.

The setting sun still lingered in the western sky, while thin trails of smoke rose from the village homes as supper was prepared.

As the familiar scenery drew near, Arvel, who had been captivated by the world outside all day, finally felt that he had come home.

The wagon continued through the village and eventually stopped in front of Darius’s general store.

“All right, we’re here.”

Darius climbed down from the driver’s seat.

Arvel and Tora jumped down from the wagon bed and reached for the crates stacked there.

“So, should I carry this one?”

“I’ll take this one.”

“No, no. It’s late, so you two go home.”

“But we helped this morning, didn’t we?”

“That was this morning. The sun’s already going down. I can handle this myself.”

Tora still looked as though he wanted to help, but Arvel glanced at the book in his arms.

“...Then shall we go home?”

“You just want to hurry up and read that book.”

“Yeah.”

Darius laughed aloud.

“Darius, thank you for today.”

Arvel bowed his head.

“Me too! It was fun!”

“Sure. Let’s go again sometime!”

“Really!?”

“Yeah!”

After thanking Darius once more, the boys left the general store.

Arvel headed home after parting with Tora along the way.

“See you, Arvel!”

“Yeah. See you tomorrow.”

Arvel watched Tora run off with a wave, then continued home himself.

◇

“I’m home!”

The moment he opened the door, warm air and the delicious smell of supper drifted out to greet him.

“Welcome home.”

Salis’s voice came from farther inside.

Then, at once—

“Arvel!”

Milika came running with the patter of quick footsteps.

“Welcome home! What did you buy!?”

“Huh?”

“In Dorgaf! You bought something, didn’t you?”

Her eyes shining, she tried to peek at what Arvel was carrying.

Arvel happily held up the book as if he had been waiting for her to ask.

“This!”

He turned the cover toward Milika.

“The second volume of The Tale of Hero Klaus!”

“...Oh. That’s all.”

The interest vanished from Milika’s face in an instant.

“What?”

“It’s another book.”

“Another...? This is what I went there to buy.”

“I thought you might have brought back sweets or something interesting.”

“I didn’t.”

“Oh...”

That was the second time.

Still clutching his precious book, Arvel looked at his sister with faint dissatisfaction.

“You two, supper is almost ready.”

Salis called to them.

“Okay!”

Milika, who had been so curious about her brother’s purchase only moments ago, now ran eagerly toward supper.

Arvel put the book in his room before going to the table.

◇

Supper was already laid out on the table.

Arvel and Milika took their seats, and Salis sat down as well.

After the four of them, including Kieval, had been eating for a while, Arvel remembered what had happened earlier that day.

“Hey, Father.”

“Hm?”

“Do you know about demon swords?”

Kieval stopped eating.

“Demon swords...”

After looking thoughtful for a moment, he turned to Arvel.

“What’s this? Have you finally discovered an interest in swords?”

“No.”

Arvel denied it at once.

“Tora found it at the bookshop today. The Complete Guide to Weapons had something about demon swords in it.”

“Tora was reading a book?”

Kieval looked slightly surprised.

“Yeah. Then we asked Darius on the way home, and he said they were swords with the power of spirits or magic in them.”

“Is a demon sword a sword?”

Milika, who had been eating until then, looked up.

“What kind!?”

Perhaps amused that his daughter had joined in as well, Kieval gave a small laugh.

“Demon swords come in many forms and kinds. Some can heal, while others are evil.”

“They’re still demon swords even if they can heal?”

Arvel asked.

“Yes. The name does not necessarily mean an evil sword. Sometimes it is used as a general term for swords with unusual powers.”

Kieval continued.

“Some simply contain magical power itself, while others have the magic stone of a spell-casting monster embedded in them. The power they possess differs from sword to sword.”

“Wow...”

Arvel stopped eating, completely absorbed in the explanation.

“Some legendary swords are even said to contain spirits.”

“Spirits!”

Arvel’s eyes immediately lit up.

Kieval laughed.

“I knew that would catch your interest.”

“What kind of spirits!?”

“The best-known example is the Sword of Flame, which is said to contain Ifrit, a spirit of fire.”

“Ifrit!?”

Arvel leaned forward even farther.

“Father, have you seen it!?”

“No.”

“What?”

“I’ve only heard stories about it myself.”

“Then you don’t know whether it really exists?”

“Who can say? At least, I’ve never seen it with my own eyes.”

“Is the Sword of Flame really burning?”

“I told you, I’ve never seen it.”

“Then is Ifrit inside the sword? Can spirits live in swords?”

“Arvel, that’s not fair!”

Milika cut in.

“Father, is that sword strong!?”

“Now, now...”

Kieval smiled helplessly as question after question flew at him.

“One at a time.”

“Then, does the Sword of Flame—”

As Arvel tried to continue questioning him, Kieval suddenly seemed to remember something.

“By the way, Arvel.”

“What?”

“Did you manage to buy the book you wanted?”

“...”

Arvel froze.

“...Oh!”

He cried out with enough force to nearly leap from his chair.

He had completely forgotten.

Why had he gone all the way to Dorgaf today?

And what was now waiting in his room?

Arvel suddenly began eating faster.

“Arvel, slow down and eat properly.”

Salis admonished him with a smile.

“But I want to read it soon.”

“The book isn’t going anywhere.”

“Okay.”

He answered, but the speed at which he ate hardly changed.

Before long, he finished the rest of his supper.

“Thank you for the meal!”

Arvel rose from his chair.

“Father! Then is the Sword of Flame stronger than an ordinary sword?”

Behind him, Milika had now begun questioning Kieval.

“That would depend on the sword.”

“Then are there swords of lightning too!?”

“Who knows? It wouldn’t be surprising if there were—”

“Then, then, could I use one!?”

“Wait, wait. I said one question at a time.”

With Kieval’s troubled voice behind him, Arvel dashed up the stairs.

His mind was already filled entirely with the newly purchased second volume of The Tale of Hero Klaus.
