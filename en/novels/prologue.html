Old Unknown Novel

Prologue

The beginning of a story set in the world of Elamnova.

Story Information

Title
Old Unknown
Chapter
Prologue

Story

A small bird flew across the clear blue sky.

It tilted its wings into the wind. Far below lay Finridge Village, bathed in the morning light.

Thin trails of smoke rose from the rooftops, and several villagers had already begun working in the fields. A cart creaked leisurely along the road while a woman carrying a basket passed beside it. Perhaps lured by the smell of breakfast drifting from a nearby house, a dog busily worked its nose.

It was a peaceful morning, no different from any other.

The little bird circled slowly above the village before eventually alighting on the roof of a house.

“Big brother! Hurry up!”

The girl's voice shattered the morning stillness. Startled, the little bird launched from the roof and fled into the sky as if in alarm.

“Can you hear me?”

“I hear you! I'll be right there!”

An answer came from inside the house. “You said that before!”

Standing in the yard, Milica puffed out her cheeks as she looked up at the second-floor window.

Nearby, her father, Kieval, was inspecting three wooden swords. He picked one up and gave it a light swing to test the grip, then laid it at the edge of the yard with the other two.

“Dad, let's start already.”

“When all three of you are here.”

“But my brother isn't coming at all.”

“He'll be here soon.”

“You said that before too.”

Kieval had no reply and gave a wry smile.

Meanwhile, in his room, Arvel was trying to close the book he had been reading.

He was trying to close it.

Beneath the page he had lifted to insert his bookmark, part of an illustration on the next page peeked out.

It showed a bird he had never seen before.

It had long tail feathers and wings spread wide. Beside the illustration was a short note explaining that the bird lived in a faraway land.

“...So something like this really exists.”

Before he knew it, his eyes were following the words.

Three more lines.

He would go after reading just those three lines.

“Arvel.”

His mother's voice came from downstairs.

“Milica is waiting for you.”

“Ah... Yeah, I'm coming!”

He answered in a hurry and, this time, finally slipped in his bookmark.

But just before he closed the book, the final line caught his eye.

Apparently, the bird could never be seen anywhere around here.

Arvel looked at the illustration just once more.

In places he did not know, there were creatures he did not know.

Perhaps that was only natural. Even so, whenever he opened a book like this, he felt as though the world he knew grew just a little wider.

“...I'd like to see one someday.”

Murmuring softly, he finally closed the book.

Just then, a cheerful voice came from the yard.

“Good morning, Kieval!”

“Morning. You're early again today.”

“Milica! Have you started already?”

“Not yet! My brother won't come down!”

“Reading again?”

“Reading again!”

Their voices overlapped perfectly.

Arvel stood silently for a moment, still clutching the closed book.

“...I said I'm coming.”

After muttering where no one could hear, he set the book on his desk.

The dry clack of wooden swords meeting drifted through the window.

It seemed the two of them had finally given up waiting.

Arvel glanced back at the book on his desk one last time, then hurried out of the room.

◇

When Arvel stepped into the yard, Milica was just blocking Tora's descending wooden sword.

A dry crack rang out.

Tora pressed forward, and Milica braced herself just as stubbornly. Neither noticed Arvel; they were utterly absorbed in their opponent.

“Sorry I'm late.”

At his voice, Kieval turned from where he stood at the edge of the yard with folded arms.

“Finally here?”

“Yeah.”

“Was the book interesting?”

“...It was.”

Kieval laughed in exasperation.

“Points for honesty.”

Arvel smiled faintly, then looked toward the sparring pair.

Milica swept her wooden sword sideways. Tora stepped back to evade it, then immediately closed in and struck.

Milica blocked.

Once. Twice. The third time. Her wooden sword was knocked violently aside, breaking her stance.

Tora did not miss the opening.

The tip of his wooden sword lightly touched Milica's shoulder.

“That's enough!”

Kieval's voice rang out. “Ah! I was so close!”

Milica gripped her wooden sword again in frustration.

“One more time!”

“No.”

Kieval stopped her at once. “Why?”

“If you start over the moment you lose, you'll never think about why you lost.”

Milica pouted. Kieval walked over to his daughter and raised his wooden sword as though replaying the exchange.

“It wasn't only the last blow. He'd been pushing you back little by little. You watched only Tora's sword and neglected your footing.”

At that, Milica looked down at her feet.

“You don't fight with the sword alone. Use your feet. When you watch an opponent, don't look only here—”

With his wooden sword, Kieval indicated the tip of Tora's blade.

“See the whole picture.”

“...Okay.”

“You'll do better next time.”

That alone brightened Milica's expression.

“You too, Tora.”

“Me too?”

“Winning isn't the end. What were you going to do if Milica hadn't lost her balance at the end?”

“Huh?”

Tora's triumphant grin vanished.

“...Strike again?”

“That would be too late.”

“Then...”

Now Tora fell into thought.

Watching them, Arvel picked up one of the wooden swords at the edge of the yard.

He had held it countless times. He gave it a light swing, testing its familiar feel in his hand.

“Good.”

Kieval looked his way.

“Arvel is next.”

“Okay.”

“Tora.”

“Yes!”

Kieval studied Tora after his brisk answer.

Despite crossing wooden swords with Milica again and again, his breathing was hardly disturbed.

“You'll be going twice in a row. Want a short rest?”

“Don't need one!”

Tora wiped the sweat from his brow with his arm and reset his grip.

“I've got plenty left!”

“You really are energetic.”

Kieval laughed.

“What about Arvel?”

“Me?”

“Tora says he doesn't need a rest.”

“I only just got here.”

“Right, you did.”

“Dad...”

Milica burst out laughing. Even Tora laughed.

“Come on, let's begin.”

At Kieval's prompting, Arvel and Tora faced each other.

Arvel raised his wooden sword. The smile vanished from Tora's face as well.

He had always been like this.

Usually noisy whatever he did, he became astonishingly serious the instant he gripped a sword and faced an opponent.

Watching his best friend, Arvel tightened his hand on the wooden sword slightly.

“Begin!”

At Kieval's call, Tora kicked off the ground.

Fast.

Arvel knew that. They had fought many times.

Yet every head-on blow from Tora made him think it anew.

Arvel stepped back half a pace and met the wooden sword.

A dull crack sounded, and the impact ran through both arms.

He stopped it. He immediately pushed back and struck in turn. Tora blocked with ease. Sliding along it, he knocked Arvel's sword aside and attacked from the flank.

Arvel drew back. The wooden sword passed within a hair of his chest.

“Oh.”

He heard Kieval's voice.

Using the momentum of his dodge, Arvel stepped in.

Tora's side was open.

He could do it.

The instant he thought so, Tora's feet moved.

Tora shifted half a step and slipped cleanly away from the target.

“What?”

Arvel followed with his eyes, but too late.

The wooden sword smacked into his back.

“Ow!”

“That's enough!”

Kieval's voice rang out. When Arvel turned, Tora was grinning with the wooden sword resting on his shoulder.

“Yes!”

Seeing Tora's broad grin, Arvel's shoulders slumped.

“...I lost again.”

“I win again!”

“I know.”

“That was close, Arvel.”

Kieval walked over to them.

“At the end, you followed Tora's sword with your eyes, didn't you?”

“Yeah.”

“If you wait to see the sword, you won't move in time. Watch your opponent's shoulders and hips. They'll tell you where he's moving before the sword does.”

Arvel replayed the movement in his mind.

He really had watched nothing but the wooden sword.

“Think I could win if we went again?”

“Sure. Want to?”

Tora immediately raised his wooden sword.

“Don't get carried away.”

Kieval lightly rapped him on the head.

◇

By afternoon, the same gentle weather that had embraced the village since morning remained, and the great tree in the yard cast a pleasant shade.

At the wooden table beneath it, Arvel sat with a book open. It was the book he had only read partway that morning.

With every turn of the page came animals he had never seen and tales of distant lands.

Nearby, Tora was swinging a wooden sword.

“Ninety-eight... ninety-nine... one hundred!”

He lowered the sword at the end and wiped the sweat from his brow.

Arvel thought he might rest, but Tora immediately raised it again.

“One!”

Arvel looked up from his book.

“You're still going?”

“Of course.”

“Even after all that this morning?”

“Morning was morning. Now is now.”

“How do you not get bored?”

“That's rich coming from you.”

Arvel looked down at the book in his hands.

“...Fair point.”

A window of the house opened.

“You two.”

Sallis leaned out and looked across the yard.

“Aren't you going fishing today?”

“We are!”

Tora answered at once.

“Then let's go.”

“Okay.”

Arvel picked up his bookmark.

Sallis smiled softly at the window.

Just then, a man passing the house noticed Tora in the yard and stopped.

A sword hung at his waist, and he wore the simple equipment of the village watch.

“Ah, I knew I'd find you here.”

Tora turned around.

“What is it?”

“The captain wants you.”

“Ugh...”

At Tora's openly displeased face, Arvel finally looked up from his book.

“Why do you look so unhappy?”

“When Dad's looking for me, it's usually nothing good...”

“He'll be angry if you don't hurry.”

“Fine... I guess I'll go.”

Urged on by the watchman, Tora reluctantly set down his wooden sword.

Arvel waved lazily and lowered his eyes to his book again.

Tora tried to answer back, but the watchman pushed him by the shoulder and led him away.

Without seeming concerned, Arvel resumed reading.

Some time later.

“Hey, Arvel.”

He looked up at the familiar voice.

Tora had returned with his hunting sword at his waist.

“Finished your errand?”

“More like... it multiplied.”

“What does that mean?”

“Come help me get rid of some Fluffers.”

Arvel immediately returned his gaze to the book.

“I thought that was why you had a sword. You can hunt Fluffers alone. I'm at a good part.”

“There are too many. They got into the fields and are ravaging the crops again.”

“Again? They multiply so quickly.”

After a moment's thought, Arvel placed his bookmark and closed the book.

“...All right. Let's go.”

“Thanks! When they're cornered they spit gel, so dealing with them alone is a pain.”

“That stuff stinks.”

“Last time it got on my clothes, and the smell wouldn't come out even after days.”

“I remember. I didn't want you coming near me for a while.”

“Isn't that a little cruel?”

Arvel rose from his chair.

“Well, shall we go?”

“Are you even listening?”

“I am.”

Arvel glanced at the closed book.

“Let's finish quickly. I want to keep reading when we get back.”

“Good, it's settled!”

Seeing Tora instantly regain his energy, Arvel let out a quiet sigh.

◇

In the fields at the edge of the village, several Fluffers were brazenly eating the leaves of the crops.

They had fluffy white fur and long ears.

By appearance alone, they did not look like creatures capable of causing harm.

Yet young leaves had been devoured throughout the fields, and many small footprints marked the soil.

“...There are more than I expected.”

“See? I told you.”

Tora drew the sword at his waist.

Arvel surveyed the area as well.

“Where do we start?”

“With those three over there.”

Tora pointed toward the edge of the field.

Three Fluffers were grazing, still unaware of them.

“Arvel, use that usual trick to drive them toward me. I'll finish them off.”

“Got it.”

Arvel stepped forward. The Fluffers' long ears twitched.

They raised their heads.

They had noticed him.

The next instant, all three bolted at once.

Arvel kicked off the ground too.

Wind gathered around his feet. The hem of his clothes lifted softly.

Using the wind pressure he created behind him, Arvel accelerated. The distance to the fleeing Fluffers swiftly shrank, and with that momentum, he swept around to the side and blocked their escape.

The startled trio changed direction, but Tora was already waiting in their path.

“Good, come this way!”

One.

Then another.

The two brought down the Fluffers with the practiced routine.

“Hey.”

Tora spoke while searching for their next prey.

“Couldn't you have used that in our match this morning?”

“Used what?”

“That just now. The thing where the wind makes you faster.”

Arvel tilted his head.

“Why?”

“You might have beaten me if you'd used it.”

“But it was sword practice.”

“It was a match, wasn't it?”

“It was sword practice.”

“Winning is what matters.”

“Then there'd be no point in Dad teaching me swordsmanship.”

“...Is that how it works?”

“Yeah.”

Tora scratched his head, looking neither convinced nor unconvinced.

“Whatever. Next one, over there!”

A Fluffer ran across the ground a short distance away.

Arvel was about to take off after it.

“Arvel, it's heading your way!”

But—his feet stopped.

“Hey, Arvel! It's getting away!”

Arvel did not move. The Fluffer plunged into the grass and vanished.

“Come on, what are you doing?”

Tora ran over.

“Hey, Arvel. What are you—”

“Shh!”

Arvel raised an index finger to his lips.

Tora reflexively fell silent.

“Look at that.”

Arvel lowered his voice.

“...Doesn't that look bad?”

“What?”

Tora followed where he pointed.

Beyond the fields, something lay fallen behind the vegetation.

At first, it looked like a large boulder.

But it was not.

It was a carriage.

It had left the road and overturned on its side.

One wheel had come off, and what appeared to be its cargo lay scattered around it.

The wind blew.

The grass rustled, revealing even more of the carriage beyond.

“...What is that?”

The lightness vanished from Tora's voice.

The two stood motionless, staring at the wrecked carriage.

At length, Tora spoke quietly.

“Let's go tell our fathers.”

Arvel did not answer.

He simply could not take his eyes from the carriage lying beyond the vegetation.
