Episode Four: Protection

Kieval and the boys head into the forest to investigate an owlbear seen near the northern abandoned mine.

Episode Four: Protection

Story

A few days later, while they waited for the carriage to be repaired, Komel had become fast friends with Arvel and the others. Once his injuries settled, Arvel, Tora, and Milica showed him around the village and went fishing with him. A few days had been enough for all four of them.

When the repairs were finished, the day of farewell came. The carriage stood ready at Finridge's entrance. Its damaged wheel and axle had been restored, the two horses were back in harness, and the bed was loaded with herbs and medicines purchased in the village.

“We truly owe you,” Rokel said, looking over Arvel, Tora, and Milica. “Next time it will be our turn. If you ever come to Ugnel, be sure to visit our shop.”

“We will!” Tora answered.

“Take care, Komel,” Arvel called.

Komel waved from the carriage. “You too!”

Rokel climbed into the driver's seat and Pedra boarded. The horses began to walk, carrying the carriage onto the road.

“See you!” Tora shouted, waving broadly. Komel leaned from the carriage and waved again and again until it disappeared beyond the trees surrounding the village.

◇

Some time later, after the usual morning training, Arvel sat at a table in his garden with The Tale of Hero Klaus, the book Rokel had given him. Though he had already finished it, he was reading again from the beginning.

Nearby, Tora and Milica faced each other with wooden swords, still arguing over their stances long after practice had ended.

As Arvel returned his eyes to the book, Barks entered the garden. “Kieval, have you heard from Ruben?”

“No. What happened?”

Barks lowered his voice. “An owlbear has appeared.”

Kieval's expression shifted. “Here? Why?”

“No idea. Sorry for the sudden request, but can you take a look?”

“Of course. Where?”

“Someone gathering mountain vegetables saw it near the abandoned mine to the north.”

Tora immediately spoke up. “Kieval, take me with you!”

“No,” Barks answered at once.

He explained that owlbears were intelligent enough to understand human speech; old ones could even speak the common tongue, and they wielded lightning magic. They were no opponent for a child.

“But Kieval will be there!” Tora protested. Arvel closed his book. He knew the name owlbear but had never seen one. “I want to see it too.”

“This isn't a game!” Barks snapped, but Kieval stopped him.

“There is no harm in letting them accompany me. I won't let them fight. They can watch from a distance.”

Kieval looked at the boys. They could hunt a fluffer and might defeat a goblin alone. That was precisely why it was time they learned there were enemies they could not handle.

“There is no shame in fear. If you know what is dangerous, you can flee when you must. That is also something you need to survive.”

After a long pause, Barks sighed. “All right. But on two conditions: do not interfere, and obey Kieval whatever happens.”

Both boys agreed and went to prepare their equipment.

◇

On the road north, Kieval told them more. An owlbear possessed not only a massive body and tremendous strength but exceptional intelligence. It understood people, some old individuals spoke their language, and it could use elemental lightning magic. Distance alone did not make it safe.

“It uses magic too?” Tora asked uneasily.

“Yes, so do nothing on your own. That means you too, Arvel.”

Kieval told them that if they felt afraid, they need not force themselves to stand and fight.

“Isn't running away because you're scared shameful?” Tora asked.

“It is far better than dying,” Kieval replied. “There will always be someone stronger. The dangerous person is the one who cannot recognize danger. Learn to run when you should.”

They eventually reached an old mine deep in the forest. Grass crowded its entrance, showing that no one had tended it for years.

Kieval crouched to inspect flattened grass and huge tracks in the soft earth. After following the signs, he stood. “Wait here. I'll check inside. Move back where you can still see the entrance.”

His tone allowed no argument. “If I tell you to return, go straight back to the village without asking why.”

They agreed. Kieval entered the tunnel and lit a torch while Arvel and Tora watched from behind the trees.

For a while, nothing happened. Only wind in the branches broke the silence.

“Was it really there?” Arvel whispered.

“Come no closer!” A deep voice rolled from within the mine.

Tora gasped and Arvel stiffened. Kieval emerged slowly from the darkness, retreating with his right hand near his sword. Beyond him, a gigantic shadow appeared.

It was enormous, with a bear's limbs, a birdlike head rising from its shoulders, a sharp beak, and great eyes fixed upon them.

An owlbear—the monster Arvel had known only from stories—stood before him.

A crushing pressure burst from the mine. Even at that distance, with Kieval between them, fear seized both boys. Tora wore his new sword, yet could not even think of reaching for it.

Unlike them, Kieval remained still and watched the owlbear.

“You do not so much as raise an eyebrow beneath my presence,” the creature rumbled, releasing still greater pressure.

Kieval did not draw his sword. He was not truly looking at the adult, but at a small shape peering from behind its body: an owlbear cub.

“You have a young one.” The tension eased slightly from Kieval's face, and his hand dropped away from his sword. “Forgive me. I didn't know. We have no wish for a needless fight. We will leave at once.”

The owlbear said nothing. It stood shielding its cub and never took its eyes off Kieval.

Kieval backed away one step at a time. The creature did not follow or leave the mine. Once he had made enough distance, he turned to the boys. “Did you see?”

They nodded solemnly.

“Then we are going home.”

As they walked away, Arvel looked back once. The great owlbear still stood before the dark entrance, its cub peeking from behind it. The parent did not move, standing like a wall between its child and the outside world.

Arvel watched for a moment, then turned and hurried after Kieval and Tora.
