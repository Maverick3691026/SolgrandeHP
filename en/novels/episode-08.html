Episode Eight: Browsing

At a bookshop in Dorgaf, Arvel finally finds the second volume of The Tale of Hero Klaus.

Story

When they finished lunch and stepped out of the restaurant, the street was still bustling with people coming and going.

Darius glanced toward the wagon carrying their goods, then spoke to the two boys.

“Well, I've got supplies to buy now.”

“Then we'll help you too!”

Darius laughed and shook his head.

“No need. All I have to do from here is visit a few shops. More importantly, Arvel, you came here to look for a book, didn't you?”

“Oh!”

At that, Arvel looked up as though he had just remembered.

Ever since arriving in Dorgaf, the unfamiliar streets and crowds had so captivated him that he had nearly forgotten his original purpose.

“There's a bookshop up ahead. Shall I walk you there?”

“Yeah!”

The three of them set off down the street again.

After walking for a while, Darius stopped in front of a shop.

“Here it is.”

Arvel looked up at the building.

It was a shop devoted entirely to books, something Finridge did not have.

That alone made his heart race.

“I'll go finish buying the supplies. I'll come back here for you when I'm done, so the two of you stay here and look around until then.”

“All right.”

“Don't wander away from the shop on your own.”

“We know.”

Tora answered.

“Then I'll see you later.”

With that, Darius headed back down the street.

Barely waiting to see him off, Arvel reached for the shop door.

◇

When he opened it, the clamor outside receded a little.

Rows of bookcases filled the shop, their shelves holding books of every shape and size.

“Whoa... Amazing.”

Tora, coming in behind him, could not help but voice his awe.

Arvel was so absorbed in looking around the shop that he forgot to reply.

Books about history.

Books about magic.

Books describing monsters that lived in all manner of lands.

There were even books on weapons such as swords and spears, and on armor and shields.

There were books about medicinal herbs, alchemy, and distant lands as well.

Everywhere Arvel looked, he found things he did not know.

“Welcome. Take your time looking around.”

Turning toward the voice, he saw an old woman seated behind a counter at the back of the shop.

“Hello.”

When Arvel greeted her, the old woman smiled gently.

Arvel went straight to the bookcases.

He would notice a title on a spine, take the book down, open it for a moment, then put it back.

Whenever he found a book about monsters, he stopped. Whenever he found one bearing the name of an unfamiliar land, he moved on to that instead.

“Arvel, there are loads over here too.”

“Yeah.”

Even as he replied, Arvel's eyes were already fixed on another book.

Why had he come to this shop?

For a while, even that slipped entirely from his mind.

At first, Tora also looked over the shelves with curiosity.

But eventually, perhaps losing interest, he let out a long breath behind Arvel as his friend picked up yet another book.

“Hey, haven't you found it yet?”

“Huh?”

Arvel turned around.

“The Klaus book. That's what you came here to find, right?”

“Oh!”

“You forgot, didn't you...?”

Faced with Tora's exasperated look, Arvel could say nothing in reply.

“Are you looking for something?”

At some point, the old woman from before had begun watching them from the counter.

“Oh, yes.”

Arvel went over to her.

“I'm looking for the second volume of The Tale of Hero Klaus.”

“A book about Lord Klaus?”

The old woman rose from her chair and looked around the shop as though thinking.

“In that case...”

She slowly walked toward the back of the shop and stopped in front of one of the bookcases.

Reaching up to a high shelf, she took down a book.

“I'm sorry, but this is the only one I have in stock right now.”

The instant he saw the book she held out, Arvel's face lit up.

The Tale of Hero Klaus, Volume Two.

It was the very book he had been searching for.

“No, that's perfect! I'll take it!”

“All right. That will be thirty-five viala.”

“Thirty-five...”

Arvel opened the leather pouch hanging at his waist.

Checking the spending money he had brought, he took out the coins one by one.

“Thank goodness... I have just enough. Three copper coins and... five iron coins.”

He placed the coins in the old woman's palm.

“Thank you.”

After checking the coins, the old woman handed the second volume to Arvel.

Arvel received it carefully in both hands.

“By the way, are you sure this is the one you want?”

“Huh?”

“There is also a children's fairy tale about Klaus. This one has a lot more words, and it may be a little difficult for you.”

“Oh, that's all right. I've read the fairy tale many times!”

“Have you now?”

The old woman's eyes narrowed in mild surprise, and then she smiled kindly.

“Well then, take your time and enjoy it.”

“I will! Thank you very much!”

Having obtained the second volume he had come for, Arvel held the book with a satisfied look.

“Tora, let's go.”

There was no reply.

“...Tora?”

Arvel turned around, but Tora, who had been nearby moments before, was nowhere to be seen.

Still holding the book, Arvel looked around the shop.

“Where did he go...?”

He peered down the aisles between the bookcases one by one.

Then, behind a bookcase at the back of the shop, he spotted Tora.

“Tora. We're leaving.”

“...”

No reply.

Tora was holding a book, staring intently at its cover.

“Tora?”

“Hey, Arvel.”

“What?”

Without taking his eyes off the cover, Tora showed the book to Arvel.

“How do you read this?”

“Huh?”

Arvel leaned over from beside him to look at the cover.

Two large characters were written there.

—Demon Sword.
