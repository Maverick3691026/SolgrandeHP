Episode Eleven: Aspiration

From admiration for masterwork swords, the children’s aspirations turn toward an unseen city and the futures awaiting them.

Episode Eleven: Aspiration

Story

Arvel thanked Cassian, left the church with Tora and Milica, and headed for the home of Ruben, the village chief.

A smithy stood beside the house, and as they drew closer, they heard the dry ring of metal being struck.

In front of the house, Celine was taking the laundry down and placing it in a basket.

“Hello, Celine.”

When the three called out, Celine turned and smiled.

“Well, hello. What brings all three of you here?”

“We want to ask the village chief about swords.”

Tora answered.

“Swords?”

“Yeah. Demon swords and masterwork swords.”

Celine’s eyes widened slightly at that, but she soon looked toward the smithy.

“He’s been in the smithy since this morning. Go and see him.”

“Thanks!”

Tora headed for the smithy first, with Arvel and Milica following behind.

When they opened the door, air hotter than the air outside greeted them.

Tools large and small hung from the walls, while pieces of metal and materials for hilts, apparently still being worked, lay across the workbench.

Ruben, who had been working farther inside, noticed the three and looked up.

“Oh, what brings all three of you here?”

“Village chief!”

As though he had been waiting for this moment, Tora stepped forward.

“How do you make demon swords and masterwork swords?”

“...That came out of nowhere.”

After looking a little surprised, Ruben set the tool in his hand on the workbench.

“Where did you hear about those?”

“Yesterday I found something about demon swords in a bookshop. Then this morning, Kieval told us there are masterwork swords too.”

“I see.”

Ruben nodded as he stroked his white beard.

“I’m no expert on demon swords myself. If you’re asking how magical power comes to dwell in a blade, that goes beyond a blacksmith’s work alone. But I can tell you about ordinary swords and masterwork blades.”

With that, he picked up a sword lying on the workbench.

“If you want to forge a good sword, you begin by choosing the material. If the metal for the blade is poor, there is a limit to what even the most careful forging can accomplish. Harder is not always better, either. Too hard and it chips easily; too soft and it bends.”

Tora and Milica stared intently at the sword in Ruben’s hand.

“Then there are the blade’s thickness and length, the distribution of its weight. Even the length of the hilt changes how easily it can be swung. Only after fitting the guard and hilt and finishing every last part do you finally have a sword.”

“So if a skilled smith uses good materials, does it become a masterwork sword?”

Milica asked.

“It isn’t that simple.”

Ruben laughed.

“No matter how well a sword is made, it cannot show its true strength if it does not suit the one using it. Give a sword that is too heavy to someone without the strength for it, and the blade will control them. Let someone wield a light sword with brute force, and they will ruin everything that makes it good.”

“So a good sword alone isn’t enough?”

Arvel asked.

“Right. A sword only becomes useful when both blade and wielder come together.”

Ruben returned the sword in his hand to the workbench.

“People have long said that some masterwork and demon swords ‘choose their wielders.’ That isn’t all. There are even swords said to ‘reject their owners.’”

“How does a sword reject someone?” Tora tilted his head.

“Who knows? I’ve never seen it myself, so I can’t tell you that much.”

Ruben stroked his white beard.

“There are stories of people cutting a finger as they try to draw one, or catching the scabbard on their foot and falling over... Though whether the sword truly rejected them or they were simply careless, I couldn’t say.”

Ruben laughed as he spoke, then glanced at the sword on the workbench.

“But whether it is a demon sword, a masterwork blade, or an ordinary sword, treat it carelessly and neglect its upkeep, and one day it will betray its owner. It might break in the middle of a battle. Ha ha ha!”

“That’s frightening...”

Tora instinctively looked down toward the sword at his waist.

“Masterwork blades are much the same. They do not perform the same way for everyone. Some only reveal their true worth after passing into the hands of a compatible wielder.”

“So the sword chooses the person...”

Tora murmured, impressed.

Beside him, Milica looked up as though something had just occurred to her.

“Then what about Father’s sword? Was that hard to make too?”

“Kieval’s?”

Ruben stared blankly for a moment, then burst into loud laughter.

“Ha ha ha! He kept pestering me to ‘forge a sword just for me.’ It’s a miserable piece I practically made in my sleep.”

“What?!”

Milica cried out.

“Father said it cuts well and it’s sturdy.”

“Of course it is, when he uses it.”

Ruben was still laughing.

“Kieval knows how to handle a sword. He doesn’t use unnecessary force, and he almost never strikes in a way that damages the blade. How long a sword lasts can change greatly depending on how it is used.”

Ruben stroked his white beard and chuckled with amusement.

“Well, in his hands, just about any sword would become a masterwork.”

“He’s that good?”

Tora’s eyes widened.

“He is. The three of you have a fine teacher.”

At that, the three instinctively looked at one another.

“Then is it really a miserable sword?”

Milica gave him a suspicious look.

“Who knows?”

Ruben smiled evasively and reached for his tools again.

Watching him, Arvel could not believe Ruben truly thought the sword was a failure.

“But still...”

Tora returned to the subject.

“Have you ever seen a real masterwork sword, village chief?”

“I have, but they’re not something you come across often.”

“Where could we see one?”

“Let me think...”

Ruben considered the question for a moment before answering.

“If you went as far as Tzadal, I imagine you could find at least one masterwork blade.”

“Tzadal?”

Arvel reacted to the name.

The capital of the Principality of Belmosia.

He had heard the name many times, but knew almost nothing about what kind of place it was.

“Is Tzadal bigger than Dorgaf?”

Ruben could not help laughing.

“You can’t even compare it with Dorgaf. The number of people, the number of buildings, the bustle—everything is completely different.”

“That much?”

“Yes. There is a great academy for the study of magic and a school that trains knights. People gather there from all across Belmosia.”

The scenery of Dorgaf, which Arvel had visited only recently, rose in his mind.

The stone-paved streets.

The crowds passing to and fro.

The rows of shops.

For someone who had known nothing beyond Finridge, that alone had seemed an astonishingly large town.

And yet there was a place in this country with which even Dorgaf could not compare.

“...I wonder what it’s like.”

The words slipped naturally from his lips.

A city he had never seen.

There, people studied magic, others trained with swords, and there were surely more things he did not know than he could ever count.

Farther away than even Dorgaf.

Within Arvel, his interest in the world he had yet to discover grew larger once again.

After leaving the smithy, the three walked side by side along the village road at midday.

After a while, Milica spoke as though something had suddenly come back to her.

“Someday, I want the village chief to make me a sword like Father’s.”

“He said that one was a failure.”

When Tora laughed, Milica immediately puffed out her cheeks.

“But I don’t think he meant it. The village chief was dodging the question at the end.”

Arvel had felt the same. The way Ruben had laughed then did not make it seem as though he truly thought the sword was a failure.

“Then you’ll have to get even stronger.”

“I will. I’m going to be stronger than Father.”

Milica said it without the slightest hesitation, and Tora laughed with delight.

When the road leading to the Lioncourt house came into view, Tora turned to the other two.

“Hey, let’s go fishing after we eat.”

“Sure.”

Beside Arvel as he answered, Milica thought for a moment and shook her head.

“I’m going to practice with my sword, so I’ll pass.”

“You’re still going to train?”

“I’m going to get stronger. Of course I am.”

Tora gave an exasperated laugh at Milica’s retort.

By the time Arvel had finished lunch, Tora had come to fetch him.

The two headed for their usual fishing spot.

They cast their lines into the gently flowing water and sat in the shade of a tree. The wind stirred the leaves and occasionally spread small ripples across the river’s surface.

But today, Arvel’s thoughts were fixed on something other than his float.

Tzadal.

A city far larger than Dorgaf.

A place with an academy for learning magic, a school that trained knights, and more unknown things than he could count.

He could not get the name out of his mind.

Beside him, Tora had been watching his fishing line when he suddenly spoke.

“Hey, Arvel.”

“What?”

“Don’t you want to go to Tzadal?”

Arvel nodded at once.

“Yeah. I’d go right now if I could.”

“I knew it.”

Tora smiled.

“Are you going to become a mage after all?”

“Yeah. I want to be a mage, but...”

Arvel looked beyond the river.

A forest lay ahead, and beyond it, lands unknown to him stretched on and on.

“I want to travel the world.”

“The world?”

“Yeah. I want to see Tzadal, and I want to visit other cities too. I want to go to places I don’t know and see things I’ve never seen.”

Tora watched his float as if considering the idea, then spoke as casually as though it were obvious.

“Then why don’t you become an adventurer?”

Arvel repeated the word in his heart.

Adventurer.

People who left their villages and lived by taking requests as they traveled through many different lands.

It was a word he had always known, yet now it sounded somehow different.

“Yeah.”

Arvel smiled faintly.

“I think I will.”

Just then, from deep within the grove came the sound of something running toward them.
