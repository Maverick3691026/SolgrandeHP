Story

When they arrived at the church, several children were already seated at their desks.

The aged church walls were decorated with paintings of every size. Some depicted myths and saints, while others showed flowers brightening the forest or the snow-covered landscape of Finridge.

Every one of them had been painted by Cassian, the priest of this church.

The morning breeze drifted through the open windows and gently stirred the papers spread across the desks.

“You're late!”

Milica called out the moment she spotted them.

“We couldn't help it. We stopped by the clinic.”

When Arvel answered, Tora nodded vigorously beside him.

“That's right. We've been busy. After all, we saved someone yesterday!”

“Why don't we leave the conversation there, you two?”

They turned at the gentle voice and found Cassian smiling at them.

“Make sure you study hard enough to make up for being late.”

“Okaaay.”

The two took the empty seats.

Finridge Village had no formal school. Three times a week, the village children gathered at the church, where Cassian taught them reading, writing, common knowledge, and morals.

Arvel looked over the assignment he had been given and immediately began working.

Characters steadily filled the page beneath his hand.

Beside him, meanwhile—

“Ugh...”

Tora clutched his head.

A short distance away, Milica had also stopped writing and was still glaring at her paper.

“Why do so many letters have to look almost the same...?”

Milica grumbled under her breath.

Arvel glanced at the two of them, smiled faintly, and lowered his eyes to his paper again.

A while later, Arvel finished the assignment and looked up.

The groaning he had heard from beside him was gone.

Tora rested his cheek on one hand, his eyes closed.

At some point, Cassian had come to stand in front of him.

“Tora, wake up.”

“...I'm awake.”

“People with their eyes closed are generally said to be sleeping.”

Giggles rose from the children around them.

At last, Tora raised his head and rubbed his sleepy eyes.

“Father, we went to the clinic first thing this morning.”

Arvel came to his defense.

“Yes, I heard. You two did something admirable yesterday, didn't you?”

Cassian gave them a gentle smile that acknowledged what they had done.

“But that does not mean you may sleep through your lessons.”

As he spoke, Cassian picked up Arvel's paper. His expression grew slightly impressed as he checked the assignment.

“You've completed everything.”

“Yeah.”

“This level of reading and writing is already too easy for you, Arvel.”

“What?!”

Milica was the first to cry out.

“I'm not even halfway done yet!”

“That's why you need to study properly.”

“That's not fair! You're the only one who can do it!”

“How is that my fault?”

The church filled with the children's laughter.

◇

Early that afternoon,

Arvel returned home and placed a book on the desk in his room.

It was the first volume of The Tale of Hero Klaus, which Rokel had given him that morning as thanks.

Arvel had heard the tale of Hero Klaus countless times since he was very young.

Klaus was a boy who lost both parents to war. Taking up the sword his father had left behind, he trained relentlessly and eventually joined the army. His rare gifts for swordsmanship and strategy brought him victory after victory, until people came to call him a hero.

The story began in the north of the Brenoria Continent,

in an age when Ernest, which would later become a great nation, was still a small principality.

Young Klaus lived peacefully with his loving father and mother in the little village of Engosa, on the shore of crimson Lake Lacast.

But in the winter of his eighth year,

war broke out along the principality's northern border, and his father was conscripted.

—I will be back soon.

His father left home with those words, but never appeared before Klaus again.

Arvel turned the page.

One page, then another.

He had heard the story countless times, yet reading the words and studying the illustrations made it feel as though he were encountering it for the first time.

Before he knew it, he could no longer hear the villagers outside or the wind passing his window.

How long had he been sitting there?

“Arvel.”

His mother's voice rose from downstairs.

“What is it?”

“Dinner will be ready soon. Would you go get your father? He should be at Hearthroot.”

“Okay.”

Arvel answered, marked the page where he had stopped, and closed the book.

◇

Hearthroot was the village's only tavern and inn.

When Arvel opened the door, he heard villagers talking amid the mingled scents of alcohol and food.

The first floor held a tavern with about twenty seats, while the second had six guest rooms for travelers. It was by no means a large inn, but it served as a gathering place for travelers seeking medicinal herbs and villagers relaxing after work.

“Good evening, Marta.”

“Oh, Arvel. Good evening.”

After Marta, the proprietress, returned his greeting, Arvel looked around the room.

He found his father at once.

Kieval sat at a table in the back, facing Barks.

Rokel was seated at the same table with them.

His son Komel sat beside him.

Cups of ale stood before the three adults.

“Dad.”

“Hmm?”

Kieval turned.

“Mom says dinner will be ready soon, so you should come home.”

“Is it that late already?”

Just as Kieval set down his cup, the tavern door flew open.

“Dad! Mom says dinner's ready, so get home!”

Tora had burst inside.

“Oh, Arvel.”

“You too, Tora?”

“Did you come to get your dad?”

“Yeah. Mom asked me to.”

“Me too.”

They looked at each other. Apparently, they had arrived to fetch their fathers at the same time.

“Well, looks like both of you have come to collect your fathers.”

Rokel laughed cheerfully.

“Sorry, Arvel. Let me finish this, then we'll go home. Have a seat and wait a moment.”

“Okay.”

At Kieval's invitation, Arvel sat in an empty chair. Tora took a seat beside Barks.

Alongside the drinks, several dishes seasoned with herbs were arranged on the table.

“By the way, Rokel,”

Kieval began.

“Yesterday got so chaotic that I never had the chance to ask. What did you come all the way to Finridge to buy this time?”

“Medicinal herbs and potions.”

Rokel answered and tipped his cup.

“Can't you get those in Ug-Nel?”

Barks asked.

“Of course I can. But circumstances have been changing lately.”

Rokel set his cup back on the table.

“There have been more skirmishes along the border with Sinestadia.”

Kieval's expression shifted slightly.

He had once served in the army of the Principality of Bermouthia and commanded the defense force facing Sinestadia. The name was all too familiar to him.

“Have there really been that many?”

“At least in Ug-Nel, we've been seeing more wounded people. Naturally, that means medicinal herbs and potions sell. Especially high-quality ones with strong healing effects.”

A smile touched Rokel's lips.

“When demand rises, a merchant sees an opportunity. Better to secure suppliers now than scramble for stock after shortages begin.”

“I see.”

Barks nodded, impressed.

As the adults continued talking, Arvel looked toward Komel, who sat beside Rokel.

“Komel, are your injuries all right now?”

At the question, Komel nodded at once.

“Yeah. I'm fine now.”

He looked more energetic than he had at the clinic that morning as he answered with a smile.

“I said it this morning too, but thank you, really. I don't know what would have happened if you two hadn't found us.”

“We only found you.”

Tora sounded slightly embarrassed.

“But if you hadn't found us there, help would never have come.”

“Well... if you put it that way.”

Despite his words, Tora looked pleased.

Arvel remembered the scene from the day before.

The overturned carriage off the road. The broken wheel and luggage scattered around it.

Then a question occurred to him.

“By the way, Komel.”

“What?”

“Why did the carriage overturn out there?”

“Oh, I wanted to ask that too.”

Tora leaned forward.

Komel looked upward as if gathering his memories.

“We were traveling along the road when a wild boar suddenly charged out.”

“A wild boar?”

“Yeah. The horses were startled and suddenly panicked...”

Moving his hands, Komel recalled what had happened.

“Dad was holding the reins, but the horses went wild, and the carriage started shaking terribly... That's when Pedra shielded me. Then the carriage veered off the road. I don't remember much after that... When I woke up, I was at the clinic.”

“So that's why the two horses that pulled the carriage were gone. They ran off then.”

Tora nodded in understanding.

“Probably. Dad told me later that they found both of them.”

“That's good.”

“Yeah.”

Komel smiled.

Arvel pictured the carriage lying beyond the grass the day before.

Yesterday it had looked like nothing more than a broken carriage on its side, but apparently all of that had happened before it came to rest there.

“One wild boar caused all that.”

At Tora's murmur, Komel gave a small nod.

Beside them, the adults' conversation continued.

“So that's why you came all the way out to this remote village.”

“Finridge's medicinal herbs are excellent. The potions made here also have stronger healing effects than ordinary ones. However far the journey, they are worth coming for.”

The medicinal herbs gathered in Finridge had long been known for their quality. They were carried to Tsadar and other places throughout the principality, where alchemists and apothecaries used them as ingredients for medicines and potions.

Arvel listened silently to the adults.

Sinestadia.

The border.

Skirmishes.

To a boy living in Finridge, all of them felt like events in some faraway place.

In the book he had been reading only a short while ago, war had completely changed Klaus's life.

But that was an old story.

The skirmishes along the border did not seem like anything that could change their own lives.
