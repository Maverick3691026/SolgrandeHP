Episode Two: The Sword and the Fairy Tale

Tora receives a sword from the merchant they rescued, while Arvel receives a volume of an old fairy tale.

Story

There was one fewer person than usual at dinner that evening.

Roasted meat, stewed vegetables, and bread that was still steaming had been set on the table.

Across from Arvel, Milica had puffed out her cheeks with a mouthful of bread. Beside her, Sallis glanced at the empty chair.

“Dad still isn't home.”

Milica spoke while chewing.

“Cleaning up the road will take time. He may be a little late.”

Sallis said this and continued eating as calmly as ever.

Arvel also ate while thinking back on what had happened that afternoon.

The overturned carriage. The scattered luggage. And that faint voice.

Barks had told him that all three travelers were safe. Even so, he did not know what had happened afterward to the two who had been unconscious.

Just as they were finishing their meal, the front door opened.

“I'm home.”

“Dad!”

Milica reacted first and sprang from her chair.

When Kieval appeared a moment later, he looked a little more worn out than he had that morning. Dirt marked his clothes in several places, and dried sweat remained on his brow.

“Welcome home. You're very late.”

“Yeah. Cleaning everything up took longer than I expected.”

Kieval pulled out a chair and finally sat down.

Sallis immediately prepared his meal and asked,

“Did you finish everything?”

“Yeah. It took longer than I expected, but all three of them are fine. The two who were unconscious woke up too.”

Arvel looked up at once.

“Really?”

“Yeah. I stopped at the clinic after we finished cleaning up. None of them was seriously injured. They've got bruises and cuts, but Céline said there was nothing to worry about.”

“Thank goodness...”

Arvel let out a quiet breath.

He had found himself wondering more than once whether things might have been different if he had noticed them sooner.

“What about the luggage?”

Arvel asked as he continued eating.

“We recovered almost all of it. Everyone took a cart back to the road. The carriage was damaged, but we managed to bring it to the village. Ruben is looking at it now.”

“Can he fix it?”

“Not right away. One wheel had come off, and several other places were damaged too. Ruben said it would probably take a few days.”

Then Kieval added as though he had just remembered,

“Oh, that's right. We found the horses too.”

“The two that were pulling the carriage. They were a little farther down the road, peacefully grazing together in a meadow.”

“Seriously...?”

Arvel made a face as he imagined the horses behaving so casually.

Kieval laughed.

“Well, the horses are safe, almost all the luggage is safe, and all three people are safe. That's enough, isn't it?”

“Yeah.”

The weight that had lingered in his chest since the afternoon finally seemed to disappear.

Reaching for the meal that had been set before him, Kieval looked at Arvel.

“That's right, Arvel.”

“What?”

“Go to the clinic with Tora tomorrow.”

“The clinic?”

“Those three want to see you. They'd like to thank you in person for saving them.”

“But we didn't do anything.”

“You certainly did.”

Kieval tore off a piece of bread as he spoke.

“If you hadn't found them, it might have taken much longer for anyone else to do so. To those people, that's more than enough to count as saving them.”

Arvel thought for a moment, then nodded.

“Okay. I'll go with Tora tomorrow.”

Kieval gave a small nod and finally began his late dinner.

◇

The next morning, Arvel and Tora went to the clinic together.

As though the commotion of the day before had never happened, another ordinary day had begun in Finridge. Familiar villagers exchanged greetings along the roadside, and livestock called in the distance.

When the clinic came into view, they saw a familiar carriage standing outside.

It was the carriage they had found overturned beside the road the day before.

The muddy body still bore vivid marks from striking a tree, and the detached wheel had been propped beside it. A man crouched in front of the carriage, peering at the axle.

It was Ruben Haldan, the chief of the village.

He wore a leather apron that seemed somewhat out of place on a village chief. With thick, knotted fingers, he touched the axle and examined the damage.

Yet no villager would have found the sight unusual.

Ruben was not only the village chief but also a blacksmith who had supported life in Finridge for many years.

“Good morning, Chief!”

Their voices rang out together.

Ruben stopped working and looked up from beneath the axle.

“Morning. You two are lively as ever.”

He smiled beneath his white beard, stood, and lightly brushed the wood shavings from his apron.

“All three are awake. Céline is inside, so go on in.”

“Okay!”

They answered together, waved to Ruben, and headed for the clinic door.

When they opened it, the fresh scent of medicinal herbs gently tickled their noses.

Inside, Céline was speaking with the three travelers.

“Oh, there you are.”

Noticing Arvel and Tora, Céline gave them her usual gentle smile.

“Good morning, Céline.”

“Morning!”

When the two greeted her, all three travelers turned toward them.

They were the same three who had lain beside the road the day before.

One had a bandage around his head, and another had cloth wrapped around an arm. Unlike yesterday, however, all three could stand on their own and speak.

Seeing them, Arvel felt the last knot in his chest finally begin to loosen.

“You're the ones...”

The merchant was the first to speak.

“You're the boys who found us yesterday, aren't you?”

“Yeah!”

Tora answered energetically, and Arvel gave a small nod beside him.

The man stepped in front of them and bowed deeply.

“Thank you. I don't know what would have happened if you hadn't found us.”

“We only found you. Our dads were the ones who saved you.”

“Even so. If you had not alerted them, that help would never have come.”

With that, the man looked at them again.

“My name is Rokel. I come from Ug-Nel, about three days north of Finridge by carriage. This is my son, Komel. We owe you both a great deal.”

The boy who had been introduced bowed a little shyly.

“I'm Komel. Thank you so much for yesterday.”

“I'm Arvel.”

“And I'm Tora!”

“And this is—”

“Pedra.”

The last of the three introduced herself before Rokel could.

“I am their escort. You have my thanks.”

Her words were brief, but her gratitude was unmistakable.

Once the introductions were over, Rokel clapped his hands as though remembering something.

“That's right. I want to reward you.”

“A reward?”

“Yes. You remember the luggage you helped carry yesterday? Choose anything you like from it, one item each.”

“Really?!”

Tora was the first to shout.

“Wait, Tora...”

Arvel tugged at his sleeve.

“What? He said we could.”

“But...”

“There's no need to hold back.”

Rokel laughed.

“In fact, I won't be satisfied unless you let me do at least this much.”

Arvel looked uncertainly at Céline, but she smiled and nodded.

“You should accept. I'm sure it will make Rokel happy too.”

“...Okay.”

“Barks and the others brought the luggage in yesterday. This way.”

Céline led the boys outside with Rokel.

They headed to Ruben's smithy next door.

The workshop had stood open since morning. In a storeroom beside its rear wall, the crates and burlap sacks recovered from the carriage had been gathered together.

Rokel pulled several crates toward them and opened the lids one by one.

“Go on. Choose whatever you like.”

“All right!”

Tora eagerly peered into the luggage.

There were fabrics, tableware, leather goods, small items, and sundries. Several books and weapons lay among them as well.

Tora's eyes stopped on one item.

“This!”

He reached for it without hesitation.

He lifted out a longsword in its sheath.

“Are you sure that's what you want?”

“Yeah!”

Tora answered with a radiant smile.

He raised it in both hands, sheath and all, and gazed at it happily.

“What about you, Arvel?”

“I...”

Prompted, Arvel looked through the goods as well.

But he could not bring himself to reach for anything.

Knowing that all of it was Rokel's merchandise made him reluctant to accept something.

Then a book caught his eye.

It was a fairly thick volume tucked between some of the other goods.

He picked it up and read the cover.

The Tale of Hero Klaus, Volume One

It was a fairy tale about the adventures of Hero Klaus, known throughout the Brenoria Continent. Arvel had heard it countless times since childhood and had even read it in books.

Yet the volume in his hands was slightly different from the ones he knew.

When he opened the cover, an illustration he had never seen before met his eyes.

He turned another page.

There was another unfamiliar illustration.

It was the same story, yet it felt like a book he had never read.

A faint light appeared in Arvel's eyes.

“...I want this.”

“That one?”

Rokel looked at the book Arvel held and raised his eyebrows uncertainly.

“That's only the first volume. I don't have any of the later ones... Are you still sure?”

“Yeah. I want this.”

“Are you certain something like that is enough?”

“I want this!”

“I want this!”

Their voices overlapped perfectly.

After a moment of silence, Rokel burst out laughing.

“Ha ha ha! I see, I see. Then both of them are yours.”

Tora hugged the sword, and Arvel held the book against his chest.

They had chosen completely different things.

Even so, the same kind of smile shone on both their faces.

“Oh.”

Céline, who had been watching them, suddenly looked up at the sky.

“Isn't it almost time for you two to go to the church?”

“What?”

They looked at each other.

“Oh!”

“Crap!”

They hurriedly adjusted the things in their arms.

“Thank you, Rokel!”

“Thank you very much!”

“No. Thank you both, truly.”

The boys bowed to Rokel and the others once more, then ran toward the church.

Tora carried a sword in his arms.

Arvel held a book against his chest.

Each carefully carrying a treasure he had not possessed the day before.
