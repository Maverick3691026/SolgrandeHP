Overview

A system of magic that resonates with earth, water, fire, wind, and other elements in nature, drawing out their power and manifesting it as varied phenomena.

It is the most widely recognized form of magic in Heiramnova and is fundamental to offense, defense, and many other uses.

Because the caster's mana serves as the catalyst, its use requires a certain quantity and concentration of mana.

Unlike utility magic, it is generally learned from someone trained in the magical arts or through systematic study at an academy or similar institution.

The attributes each caster finds easiest to handle differ, shaped by inborn aptitude, upbringing, and many other factors.

Although the caster's mana activates elemental magic, its strength and efficiency depend greatly on affinity with the elements found in nature.

Characteristics

The Four Great Elements and Derived Attributes

Elemental magic is founded on the four great elements of earth, water, fire, and wind.

Many derived attributes inherit the qualities of these four elements and develop into diverse spells according to the caster's aptitude and purpose.

Affinity with Nature

Elemental magic displays its true power through affinity with the elements present in nature.

Affinity is formed not only by inborn qualities, but also by upbringing, one's relationship with nature, faith, reverence for nature, and many other factors.

Those who respect nature and live alongside it tend to deepen their affinity. Those who destroy nature for selfish ends or repeatedly engage in needless overhunting or forest burning tend to damage it.

Effects of Affinity

Even if affinity declines, elemental magic itself does not become unusable.

Because the caster's own mana powers the spell, someone with sufficient mana quantity and concentration can still cast it.

With low affinity, however, the caster cannot fully draw out the element's natural power, leading to weaker effects, increased mana consumption, and reduced precision of control.

A caster may experience this merely as feeling out of condition, yet over time it affects combat ability and magical efficiency.

Relationship with Alchemy

Elemental magic is closely connected to alchemy.

Students of magic therefore commonly learn basic alchemical knowledge as well, including elemental properties and changes in matter.

This is foundational education intended to deepen understanding of elemental magic, not training to master alchemy itself.

Mana Consumption

Each elemental spell has a minimum required mana cost.

By supplying more than that minimum, a caster can increase power, range, duration, and other properties.

Excessive investment risks mana exhaustion or depletion, so mana must be allocated appropriately for the situation.

Low elemental affinity can also raise the cost of the same spell and prevent it from reaching its natural performance.

Learning Difficulty

Elemental magic is harder to learn than utility magic and requires sufficient mana quantity and concentration.

Those without mana can use neither elemental magic nor utility magic.

At academies, students spend roughly their first six months learning the foundations of earth, water, fire, and wind, then specialize according to their aptitude.

After choosing a specialty, they advance through beginner, intermediate, and advanced study, though those recognized for exceptional talent or effort may be allowed to skip grades.

Outside academies, many become apprentices to experienced mages and learn elemental magic through practical training.

Talent is not determined by birth alone and can grow greatly through effort and discipline.

It is said that even those able to learn only a few kinds of magic have left their names as first-rate mages by honing a single spell to its utmost limit.
