Overview

National courts are judicial institutions established and operated by each nation to maintain its domestic legal order.

They hear civil and criminal cases and have authority to pass judgment on serious crimes such as murder, kidnapping, human trafficking, and arson.

Major sentences, including penal slavery, lifelong penal slavery, and death, are imposed under each nation's laws.

Judges appointed by each nation conduct trials, and final judgments are carried out in the name of the king, emperor, grand duke, or governing institution.

Main Roles

Hearing civil and criminal cases

Trying serious crimes

Passing major sentences such as penal slavery, lifelong penal slavery, and death

Adjudicating legal disputes involving contracts, inheritance, land, and commerce

Issuing judicial decisions concerning criminals, slave traders, and illegal organizations

Penal Slavery

In Heiramnova, penal slavery is one punishment that national courts impose on serious criminals.

It applies to offenders guilty of murder, kidnapping, human trafficking, arson, and similar serious crimes; the term and disposition are decided under each nation's laws.

Conversely, enslaving ordinary citizens through kidnapping or human trafficking is a serious crime, and illegal slave traders and organizations face severe punishment by national courts.

Free City of Fenrod

Because the Free City of Fenrod has no state government, it has no court.

Disputes and crimes are judged through deliberation by the city watch and district representatives.

Serious cases are also deliberated with residents' representatives, and dispositions are decided under the city's own customary law.

Although different from a national judiciary, this system is essential to Fenrod's autonomy and order.
