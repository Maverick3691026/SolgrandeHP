Gazelio

Gazelio

A herbivorous beast-type monster with horns and scales, found in grasslands, forests, and near water.

Basic Information

Name
Gazelio
Classification
Beast-type monster
Threat Level
Low
Habitat
Forests, grasslands, and watersides such as rivers and ponds

Overview

Gazelios are herbivorous beast-type monsters that inhabit grasslands, forests, and watersides.

With excellent hearing, smell, and leg strength, they vanish in an instant upon sensing danger, making them difficult to hunt or capture.

At the same time, their horns and scales are both highly valuable, so they are widely sought after by adventurers and hunters.

Ecology

Gazelios are diurnal herbivorous monsters. They live mainly on weeds, young leaves, and plants that grow near water.

Except during the breeding season, they generally live alone or in pairs and rarely form large herds.

They are highly territorial. During the breeding season, males in particular clash horns and fight fiercely over territory.

Their keen hearing and sense of smell allow them to detect danger quickly, and they leave immediately upon sensing humans or large monsters.

However, an individual with no escape route will fight back fiercely with its two sharp horns.

Their lifespan is approximately 25 to 35 years. Large carnivorous monsters are their natural enemies. Adults are rarely caught because of their agility, but juveniles are often targeted.

Appearance

They have two sharp horns resembling polished obsidian, while hard scales cover them from the back of the head to the tail.

Their lean limbs are slender yet powerful and support their exceptional running speed.

Temperament

They are docile but highly wary. When they sense humans or large monsters, they flee swiftly.

Characteristics

Juveniles have no horns; their two horns grow over the course of several months after birth.

They normally live alone or in pairs and rarely form herds.

Males are more aggressive and territorial than females.

A charge powered by their well-developed legs is devastating, and their two sharp horns can easily pierce even iron armor.

Facing one carelessly from the front is therefore extremely dangerous.

The scales extending from the back of the head to the tail are also extremely hard, making attacks from behind less effective.

Drops

Gazelio horns, Gazelio scales, Gazelio meat, and Gazelio magic stones

The horns combine hardness and toughness and are prized as materials for spears, daggers, kitchen knives, and similar tools. Because Gazelios are difficult to hunt, the horns command high prices on the market.

The scales offer excellent protection and are used as armor materials.

Gazelio Armor in particular is popular among mid-ranking adventurers for combining strong defense with low weight.

The meat is high in protein, low in fat, and nutritious, but because of its distinctive flavor it is often smoked or grilled with herbs.

The magic stones have a low concentration of magical power. Though unsuitable for expensive magical devices, they are widely distributed as fuel for household goods and simple magical tools.

Notes

As herbivores that favor weeds and waterside plants, Gazelios play a part in maintaining the natural environment.

They are extremely wary and keenly detect human scents and presence, so they almost never approach settlements or farmland.

They are therefore rarely treated as crop-damaging pests like Fluffers.

On the other hand, their keen vigilance and agility make them difficult to hunt. Hunters say, “A cornered Gazelio is more frightening than a fleeing one.”
